Ball Party · proposal · auction team builder

The lot and the table

A counterpart to auction-current.html. Same fixtures — four-handed, you are DEV, lot 7 of 20 — the same tokens, and the same portrait band that file adopted, so the two sheets can sit side by side and be argued with directly.

The screen is now two named regions. The Lot is the thing being bid on, its price, its clock and its controls. The Table is everyone's money and rosters. Below 1024px they stack; at 1024px and above they sit side by side.

The structural claim, and it is measurable. The page never scrolls, at any width. The Table is sized by its seats; the Lot takes whatever is left; and inside the Lot the portrait band is the only element with slack. Every state change is therefore absorbed by the face and nothing else moves. Measured across all five match frames on a 390×780 phone: the Lot region is 450px in every one, the buttons sit at 422px in every one, the Table’s top edge is 531px in every one — while the portrait ranges from 147px down to 114px doing the absorbing.

The auction sequence

There are two timer-on bidding states and two timer-off transition states. Active bidder means the player who may make the next bid; high bidder means the player currently winning the lot. They can be the same person, but the labels are never interchangeable.

01 · TIMER OFFNew playerShow the player, position and scarcity for 3 seconds. Controls stay disabled.
02 · TIMER ONYou are activeYou can bid. The bright market row, large seconds and live controls say so.
03 · TIMER ONSomeone else is activeName the active bidder; keep your controls unavailable and the current high bid visible.
04 · TIMER OFFSoldConfirm the winner, price and roster destination before dealing the next player.

Mobile · 390 × 780

The same states as the current sheet, in the same order, so they can be compared one to one. Nothing scrolls except the Table. The new-player and sold beats reuse this fixed geometry while the timer is stopped.

Web · 1280 × 800 and 1280 × 720

Shown at 65% so real windows fit on this sheet. Unlike the current build these are a different layout, not the phone column centred in a wide window — one breakpoint at 1024px does all of it. W0–W3 show the complete auction sequence: preview, your active bid, another player’s active bid, and sold.

What this proposal does and does not settle

Numbered against the findings in auction-current.html, so the two lists line up.

01 · There is no web layout — closed

One breakpoint at 1024px. Below it, the stacked phone column. At and above it, Lot and Table side by side inside max-w-6xl, with the TopBar widened from max-w-5xl to match so the chrome and the game share an edge. Two layouts, not three, and no fluid middle to test.

02 · The lot is the smallest thing on screen — stays closed

The portrait band already closed this and nothing here reopens it. The band now flexes rather than sitting at a fixed 132px: it is the only element in the whole layout with slack. On a phone it runs 114–147px depending on state; on a laptop 276px in a 720px window and 356px in an 800px one. Every height decision anywhere else on the screen is paid for out of the face — which is the right place to take it from, because the face is the one thing here that degrades gracefully.

03 · The payoff is transient, and off-screen — closed

Frame 3, W3. The reveal takes the Lot region for a beat instead of being a seventh block under a live lot. Only one lot is ever live, so the two never need to share a column — which is note 03b of the current sheet, built.

Worth being explicit about a bug this also fixes. In the build, lastResolved is any lot with resolved_at set, so the reveal card is on screen permanently from lot 2 onward, showing steadily staler results. By lot 18 you are looking at lot 17. Frames 1, 4, 5 and W1 of the current sheet — a live lot with no reveal block — are states the running game cannot reach. Making the reveal a beat rather than a block is what turns it back into an event.

04 · Waiting and acting look nearly identical — closed

Frames 1 and 2. Four things change together on your turn: the market row goes cream-edged and lit, the price steps from 30px to 38px, the clock becomes a slab numeral instead of a small muted figure, and the buttons come up to full contrast. Nothing changes position, so the difference is loud without anything moving under a thumb.

05 · The layout moves while the clock runs — closed

Frame 5. The status line under the buttons is always 17px tall whether it holds a blocked reason, a raise limit, an error, or nothing. Errors replace text in a reserved slot rather than inserting a block above the controls.

The stronger guarantee, though, is structural rather than per-component. Because the Table is content-sized and the Lot takes the remainder, the Lot region cannot change height at all — not when the price grows on your turn, not when the weave becomes a number, not when an error arrives. Measured across every match frame on this sheet: Lot 450px, controls at 422px, Table at 531px, identical in all five. Nothing can move while a clock is running because there is nowhere for it to move to.

06 · Every explanation is 10px mono — closed

Three weights now. Sentences — blocked reasons, errors, what a lot would do for you — are 12px Karla, sentence case, in the reserved line. Strategic numbers — scarcity, price, budget, seconds — are cream and sized to matter. Ambient labels — “Lot 7 of 20”, “The table” — stay 10px muted mono, which is what that treatment should have been reserved for.

07 · Two hidden-information languages — closed

The weave now means exactly one thing: information that exists and is being withheld from you. That is the lot’s fantasy points, and nothing else. Empty roster slots are dashed outlines, because an empty slot is a gap rather than a secret. The timer also stops being red by default and only goes red inside the last three seconds, so urgency and concealment are no longer the same colour.

08 · New — the lot is connected to your roster

Not on the current list, but the decision a player is actually making. The status line says “Fills your open RB” when a lot is eligible, in the same slot that already explains why you are locked out when you are not. The build has careful prose for blockedReason and says nothing at all in the case where you can bid.

09 · New — a countdown you can actually read

BidBar computes seconds and spends it entirely on an aria-label. A sighted player gets a 4px bar; a screen-reader user gets the better affordance. The seconds are now on screen for everyone, and large on your own turn.

10 · New — the player arrives before the auction begins

New-player preview is a real state, not loading. For three seconds the Lot shows the incoming player, position and scarcity with the auction clock at not started. Controls are unavailable and the Table remains stable. That gives everyone the same chance to identify the player before the active-bidder state begins; it also prevents a bid from arriving while the player identity is still changing.

11 · New — selecting a player has a visible destination

Frame 3, W3. When the server accepts the winning bid, freeze the settled price and present “Sold to [player] for $[price]” for 1.2s. Highlight the winner’s seat and pulse the newly filled roster slot together. The next preview begins only after that acknowledgement. The animation is never run while the bidding timer is live, and prefers-reduced-motion keeps the winner and slot highlighted without motion.

12 · New — bid an exact amount

Frame 1, W1. Custom bid opens a non-modal popover that replaces the fixed control surface, never the price or seconds. It pre-fills the current minimum, accepts whole-dollar input between the stated minimum and affordable maximum, and exposes those limits to assistive technology. Submitting follows the same server-confirmed path as a +$1 or +$5 bid. A rejected amount leaves the popover and auction state open; its helper becomes a polite validation message while retaining the limits. Escape, Cancel, a turn change, or a sold lot closes the popover and returns focus to Custom.

State contract

The UI has four explicit states: lot_preview (timer off), bidding_self (you are the active bidder), bidding_other (another player is active), and sold (timer off). The handoff from sold to the next preview is internal, not a fifth visible state. Active bidder is who may take the next bidding action; high bidder is who would win if the clock expired. A rejected or late bid leaves the client in its last server-confirmed bidding state, shows the error in the reserved status line, and must never play the sold animation.

Costs, stated plainly

  • One reserved line means one message. Frame 5: the rejected bid outranks “you can only raise by $1”, which then rests entirely on the disabled +$5. The alternative was a line that grows and shoves the layout, which is the thing this is trying to stop.
  • Mobile roster chips lose the player name. They already had, in the build — this keeps position and points on the phone and only adds the name at 1024px. But it means the phone still cannot answer “who does Maria have at WR”.
  • The Table is capped at 46% of the column on a phone. Four seats fit inside that with room to spare. Past about five it hits the cap and scrolls internally — correct behaviour, but it means the guarantee above is precisely “the Lot never moves”, not “nothing ever scrolls”. Worth knowing the seat cap before this is built.
  • The no-scroll rule is a permanent tax on everything added later. Any new block in the Lot column comes out of the portrait, and in a 720px window there is only about 106px of slack above the 170px floor before something has to give. Bid history, a chat line, a lots-remaining strip — each one costs the face.

What is not built here

Bid history (who raised by how much, which is the read on what another player knows) remains deliberately out of scope. It is useful new product surface, but it should not compete with the fixed-height auction interaction in this change.

Also worth flagging: the photo field does not exist yet. auction_lots has no headshot column and neither does player_weeks, so the portrait band on both sheets is a design decision waiting on a migration and a source for the images.
