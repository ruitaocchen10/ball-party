Ball Party — three UI directions

Same three screens in each direction — lobby, a Wavelength guessing round, the scoring moment — so the comparison is like for like. All copy and state is real: room BLTZ, round 3 of 8, Kayla is the psychic, the clue is “Gronk”, the needle lands in a 3. Best judged on a phone.

Direction 01

The Jumbotron

The room is the arena. Your phone is a seat; the shared game state is what’s on the big board. The signature is the shot clock — one dot-matrix countdown owns the top of every screen in every game, goes amber → buzzer red, and shudders under five seconds. Your architecture says hard per-action timers in every game, so one clock treatment is what makes four unrelated minigames read as one product.

Direction 02

The Card Table

Not an app — a stack of stuff on a table between four people. Felt, a torn pack, a scoresheet someone’s keeping in pen. The signature is the pack rip: a session opens by tearing a pack, and your three opponents come out as foil-edged cards. That turns the most templated screen in every party game — four avatars in a lobby — into the most memorable three seconds of the product, and your card follows you across all four games, gaining stat-back lines as you score.

Direction 03

The Draft Room

For people who actually know ball. The war room at 7:58 — magnet nameplates on a whiteboard, a manila folder, tabular numbers that line up. The signature is the sealed folder: hidden information stays on screen for everyone as a physical object they can’t open. Invisible hidden info feels like nothing is happening; a closed folder on the table is tension — which matters because you’ve cut Wavelength’s catch mechanic, the thing that normally keeps idle players engaged.

Where I’d put my money

The Card Table. Three of your four games are card games in their mechanics, so the metaphor does structural work rather than decoration, and the persistent player card is the natural home for Party Mode’s running score. It’s warm without being exhausting over a fifteen-minute session.

Two things worth stealing across directions: the Jumbotron’s shot clock, because the timer is the one place this app should be loud and the card table handles it weakly; and the Draft Room’s sealed folder, because keeping hidden information visible-but-shut is the cheapest fix for the idle-player problem you flagged when you cut the catch mechanic.
